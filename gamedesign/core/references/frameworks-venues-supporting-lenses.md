# Design Frameworks — Venues & Supporting Lenses

Supplementary Schell lenses extracted from `frameworks.md` (load on demand when
choosing a target venue, or when grounding a design with the lighter supporting
lenses — endogenous value, surprise, curiosity, the five kinds of listening). The
source itself marks this material as "lighter" / "supporting"; it is peripheral to
the core frameworks and rarely needed on a typical design task. Source: Jesse
Schell, *The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses*, Ch 1–6.

## Venues & the Designer's Core Skill (Schell, lighter)

- **Venues** (Schell's *Lens of the Venue*): the *place* where play happens shapes
  the design more durably than the platform — platforms churn, venues barely
  change. Private venues (the **hearth** — shared, watchable, social living-room
  play; the **workbench** — focused, skill- and time-heavy solo/online play; the
  **reading nook** — calm, short, simple tablet-style sessions), public venues
  (theater, arena, museum), and in-between (the gaming table, the playground,
  **anywhere** — short-session, interruptible, simple mobile play). Ask: which
  venue best fits this game? What traits of that venue can I exploit? Which of my
  elements fit the venue and which fight it?
- **Listening is the designer's single most important skill** (Schell): listen
  deeply — past the surface — to **five things: the team, the audience, the game
  itself, the client, and yourself.** "Listening to the game" means knowing it so
  well you can diagnose what's wrong just by how it plays, the way a mechanic
  hears a failing engine.

## A Game as Problem-Solving (Schell, supporting)

Schell's working definition: **a game is problem-solving activity, approached with
a playful attitude.** Useful corollary lenses for grounding a design:

- **Endogenous value** (*Lens of Endogenous Value*): items, points, and currency
  matter only insofar as the game makes them matter; their felt value is a direct
  readout of how much players care about succeeding. If a collectible has no
  bearing on the player's actual goal, its value is zero and players will ignore
  it — tie rewards to what the player is actually trying to do.
- **Surprise & anticipation** (*Lens of Surprise*): **fun is pleasure with
  surprises.** The brain is wired to enjoy the unexpected (even unpleasant
  surprises light up pleasure centers). Build surprise into rules, story, art,
  and tech — and let players surprise *each other* and *themselves*. This
  complements the Flow channel: Flow regulates challenge over time; surprise
  supplies the moment-to-moment delight Flow's pacing carries.
- **Curiosity** (*Lens of Curiosity*): players are driven less by the goals you
  set than by the *questions those goals raise in their heads* ("can I beat this
  in 30 s?", "what's the next level like?"). Design to provoke and renew those
  questions.
