# Level Design — Psychology of Horror

Detailed horror-technique taxonomy extracted from `level-design.md` (load on demand
when designing a horror game or a horror level/section). The main rule keeps the
conceptual hook (the four levers); this file holds the full 13-technique checklist
with its design mechanisms. Source: Михаил Кадиков, *Проектирование виртуальных миров.
Теория и практика дизайна уровней* (Ridero, 2020).

## Psychology of Horror

A taxonomy of 13 techniques, grouped under four levers (use as a checklist):
**(1) maximize immersion, (2) pull the player out of the comfort zone, (3) strip
situational control, (4) make the player vulnerable.**

| # | Technique | Design mechanism |
|---|-----------|------------------|
| 1 | **First-person view** | "from the eyes" maximizes identification; an external avatar dilutes fear (*Alien: Isolation*, *RE7* both moved to first person) |
| 2 | **Invade personal space** | intrusion into personal space always discomforts (strongest in VR); make it intrinsic to mechanics (enemies leaping into lethal "embraces") |
| 3 | **Discomfort zone** | invert the want-to-revisit goal — make the player want to flee; exploit phobias and **disgust** (filth, insects, rot), force physical contact with revolting things; half-recognizable distorted human enemy forms shock most |
| 4 | **Loss of control** | dark tangled mazes, many forks/ambush points; force the player's back to doors/windows; snake/zig-zag corridors + view-blockers so the threat can't be tracked at range |
| 5 | **Unpredictable enemy** | genuine non-scripted AI or skilled event direction casts the player as prey (*Alien: Isolation*'s vent-using algorithm; *Prey*'s object-mimicking Mimics) |
| 6 | **"Fear has big eyes"** | in low visibility, layered ambient sound lets imagination fill gaps with the worst image — merely *hinting* at horror makes players scare themselves; returning to a place of bad memories re-triggers dread |
| 7 | **False alarm** | constant stress dulls emotion — periodically contrast danger and safety with harmless jump-scare bait so tension can discharge |
| 8 | **Vulnerable player** | deny effective melee; limit weapons, reload speed, ammo, medkits — each fight sharpens, movement turns cautious |
| 9 | **Invulnerable enemy** | an unkillable, ever-pursuing monster forces panic and turns trivial tasks (lockpicking, waiting for a lift) into high-tension setpieces |
| 10 | **Agonizing wait** | make key actions deliberately slow (manual reloads, lockpicking, calling the lift) so the player is exposed throughout |
| 11 | **Cost of failure** | failure must carry a high, vividly-depicted price (grievous injury, brutal detailed death) |
| 12 | **Nowhere to run** | deny any fully-safe refuge (even the locker isn't safe; the home safe-room is gradually invaded) |
| 13 | **Loss of progress** | scarce/effortful manual saves (finite ink ribbons; a save terminal with an exposed activation delay) make progress-loss a real fear |
